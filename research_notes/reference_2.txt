Classification of Roasted Coffee Bean Maturity 
Levels Using Convolutional Neural Network with 
Grad-CAM Visualization 
 
 
Abstract—Determining the maturity level of roasted coffee 
beans is an important step because it can affect the quality of 
coffee production in Indonesia. Therefore, this study was 
conducted to develop a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) 
model using EfficientNet -B0 to classify the degree of maturity 
and apply the Gradient -weighted Class Activation Mapping 
(Grad-CAM) visualization technique. The data used consisted 
of images of roasted coffee beans categorized into four degrees 
of maturity, namely green, light, mediu m, and dark. The test 
results show that the model is capable of achieving an accuracy 
of more than 98% with a loss value below 0.20. This indicates 
that the model has reached optimal convergence without 
overfitting. In addition, the heatmap visualization f rom Grad-
CAM proves that the model focuses on relevant visual areas, 
such as the color and texture of coffee beans, in determining 
classification decisions. 
Keywords—Deep Learning, Convolutional Neural Network, 
EfficientNet-B0, Grad-CAM, Roasted Coffee Bean 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Coffee production in Indonesia in 2024 reached 789,233 
thousand tons, an increase of 3.86% from 2023 [1]. As one of 
the largest coffee producing countries, Indonesians have 
made drinking coffee a part of their culture. The habit of 
enjoying coffee is not only ingrained in daily activities, but 
also part of social interactions, lifestyle, and even the identity 
of the younger generation. Drinking coffee every day has 
even become an inseparable routine for most people. Based 
on GoodStats survey [2], as many as 40% of Indonesians 
admit to consuming two cups of coffee per day, indicating a 
high level of dependence and demand. 
The increase in demand for coffee products needs to be 
balanced with efforts to maintain the quality of coffee beans 
so that quality stability is maintained. Therefore, identifying 
the maturity of coffee beans is a crucial stage in the coffee 
production ch ain. In order to maintain coffee quality, 
computer vision technology through the Convolutional 
Neural Network (CNN) architecture offers an objective 
automation solution. CNN is capable of learning complex 
visual patterns such as color and texture to classi fy coffee 
fruit ripeness with high accuracy, replacing inefficient 
manual processes. Although CNN excels in accuracy, this 
model is often considered a black box because its decision -
making process is difficult to understand.  Therefore, this 
research aims not only to build a CNN model for coffee fruit 
ripeness classification, but also to apply the Gradient -
weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad -CAM) 
visualization technique. The use of Grad -CAM will produce 
a heatmap to analyze and visualize the model's foc us, 
resulting in a classification system that is not only accurate 
but also transparent and interpretable. 
II. LITERATURE REVIEW 
A. Previous Study and Research 
Research on the classification of coffee bean 
roasting levels has been conducted using various 
approaches based on image processing and deep learning. 
One relevant study is Coffee Roast Intelligence. This 
study utilizes Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) to 
identify the maturity level of roasted coffee beans. CNN 
was chosen as the primary method due to its ability to 
extract complex visual patterns such as the color and 
texture of roasted coffee beans [3].  
In the Coffee Roast Intelligence study [4], there was 
no model that utilized explainable AI to identify factors 
that influence the classification decisions of the methods 
used. To support this need for interpretability, this study 
integrated the Gradient -weighted Class Activation 
Mapping (Grad -CAM) method as a visualization tool 
capable of showing which areas of the coffee bean image 
are the main focus of the model when determining the 
roasting level. Grad-CAM has been proven to be suitable 
as a method of interpretation and transparency for CNN 
in research conducted by Ramprasaath [5]. This approach 
is expected to not only increase model transparency, but 
also make it easier for users such as roasters, quality 
control, and coffee industry players to visually validate 
prediction results. 
B. Deep Learning 
An example of artificial intelligent technology is 
Deep Learning. Deep Learning is a machine learning 
method used for object identification and classification 
[6]. Deep Learning methods utilize non -linear 
transformations and multi -level abstraction to handle 
large data sets [7]. In 2006, the concept of Deep Learning 
emerged as a new branch of research in the field of 
machine learning [8].  
 
C. Convolutional Neural Network  
Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is one of the 
most efficient and commonly used deep learning methods 
in visual data processing, such as image recognition and 
video analysis [9]. The CNN architecture is designed to 
mimic the workings of the visual cortex in the human 
brain, where neurons respond to stimuli in specific 
receptive fields to distinguish between different objects 
[10]. Basically, the CNN structure consists of several 
main layers, namely the input layer, the convolution layer 
for feature extraction, the pooling layer to reduce spatial 
dimensions and computational complexity, and the fully 
connected layer for final class ification [11] . The main 
advantage of CNNs over conventional methods is their

ability to perform feature extraction automatically 
without requiring separate extraction tools, enabling them 
to gradually learn raw data representations from basic to 
complex patterns. 
D. EfficientNet-B0 
EfficientNet-B0 is the base model of the 
Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture series 
known as EfficientNet. This architecture is designed to 
achieve an ideal balance between high accuracy and 
computational efficiency. Unlike conventional CNN 
models that manually scale network dimensions, 
EfficientNet applies a compound scaling technique that 
systematically scales the depth, width, and resolution of 
the network using fixed compound coefficients [6], [7]. 
The main architecture of EfficientNet -B0 was developed 
using Mobile Inverted Bottleneck Convolution blocks, 
inspired by MobileNetV2. However, these blocks were 
developed with a Squeeze -and-Excitation (SE) 
mechanism to improve feature extraction, as well as 
replacing the ReLU activation function with Swish [6], 
[12]. 
E. Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping 
Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping 
(Grad-CAM) is a visualization technique developed to 
improve the transparency and interpretability of deep 
learning models, particularly Convolutional Neural 
Networks (CNNs), which are often considered black 
boxes [13]. This method works by utilizing gradient 
information from the target class flowing to the last 
convolutional layer, thereby producing a coarse 
localization map, which is then visualized as a heatmap 
[14], [15]. This heatmap intuitively emphasizes important 
areas or pixels in the input image that most influence the 
model's prediction decision, allowing users to check 
whether the model focuses on relevant object features or 
only learns inappropriate background patterns [16]. In the 
context of image classification, Grad -CAM provides 
post-hoc visual explanations that help domain experts 
assess model performance by displaying high visual 
intensity in areas that form the basis for classification 
decisions. Although the resulting  interpretation is 
qualitative, this approach is very useful for ensuring that 
the classification model has learned the correct 
discriminative features of the detected objects [15].  
F. Coffee 
According to the Badan Pusat Statistik, Indonesia 
was able to produce 758.72 tons of coffee in 2023 [17]. 
This is also influenced by the high demand for coffee 
among the public, as drinking coffee has become a way 
of life in Indonesia. Therefore, Indonesia ranked first in 
coffee consumption in Southeast Asia in 2019 [18].  
To produce coffee beans that can be used to make 
drinks, coffee must undergo a roasting process. This 
technique is the key to coffee's aroma because roasting 
causes the coffee beans to expand and open up [19]. 
Coffee bean roasting levels are divided into four 
categories: green, light, medium, and dark   [20]. The 
levels range from the lightest to the most intense. 
III. METHODOLOGY 
The main method used to classify the maturity level of 
coffee beans is Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). In 
order to improve transparency and clarify the decision -
making basis of the CNN model, this study utilizes the Grad-
CAM visualization technique. Through the analysis of the 
heatmap generated by Grad-CAM, it is able to identify areas 
in the image that contribute most significantly to the model's 
prediction. The complete process and workflow of the 
method used in this study are presented in Figure 1.

A. Data Preparation 
The dataset used in this study consists of images of 
roasted coffee beans obtained from the Kaggle at Coffee 
Bean Dataset Resized (224 X 224) [21]. The dataset consists 
of four categories of maturity levels, namely green, light, 
medium, and dark. All images have variations in lighting 
conditions, shooting angles, and backgrounds, so a pre -
processing stage is required before the data can be used as 
input for the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model. 
All images are then read using OpenCV, which by default 
loads images in BGR format. Therefore, each image is 
converted to RGB format to comply with the input standard 
of the EfficientNet architecture used in this study. Upon 
completion, the images are resized to 224 × 224 pixels using

the resize function. This resolution normalization is 
necessary to maintain input dimension consistency and 
ensure compatibility with the pretrained model type 
employed. The processed images are then stored in a data 
structure containing image pairs and cl ass labels, then 
converted into a four-dimensional NumPy array (N, 224, 224, 
3), where N is the total number of samples. Once all the data 
has been collected, randomization is performed to eliminate 
sequence bias and prevent the model from learning based o n 
sequential class patterns. 
The final stage of pre-processing is separating the dataset 
into training data and testing data. The separation is done to 
evaluate the model's performance on data that was not used 
during the training process, so that the evaluation results are 
more objective and not affected by overfitting. Overall, this 
stage ensures that all images have been standardized and are 
ready to be used in the training process of the EfficientNetB0-
based CNN model with Grad-CAM visualization.

classification head, and training parameter configuration.

towards the global optimum point.

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
In this section, testing and discussion will be carried out 
on the coffee bean roasting maturity classification system that 
has been developed. The testing was carried out by analyzing 
the prediction results of the CNN model and Grad -CAM 
visualization. The training results data were presented in the 
form of accuracy and loss graphs to see the model's 
convergence level at each e poch, as well as a confusion 
matrix to see the details of classification errors between 
classes. The Grad-CAM visualization approach was also used 
to prove that the model actually learned relevant visual 
features, such as the color and texture of coffee beans. 
Based on the prediction results shown in Figures 2, 3, 4, 
and 5, the model successfully identified the maturity level of 
coffee beans accurately. In the Green, Light, and Medium 
categories, the model shows a very high confidence level 
of >90%. Meanwhile, i n the Dark category, the model 
provides a convincing prediction result of 80%, which 
indicates that the visual features of dark brown color and 
texture in roasted beans are well recognized by the model.

V. CONCLUSION 
Based on the results of testing and analysis that have been 
carried out, it can be concluded that the Convolutional Neural 
Network (CNN) model developed is capable of classifying 
the maturity level of coffee beans with excellent 
performance. The Grad -CAM heatmap results show that the 
model focuses on areas of the image that are visually relevant, 
such as color, darkness intensity, and coffee bean surface 
texture, ensuring that the model's decisions do not depend on 
random artifacts but rather truly learn th e important features 
of the object. Quantitatively, the model shows a significant 
increase in accuracy at each epoch and achieves a final 
accuracy value of over 98%, accompanied by a steady

decrease in loss to a value below 0.20. This confirms that the 
model has reached an optimal convergence point without 
experiencing overfitting. The Confusion Matrix visualization 
also shows the dominance of true positive values on the main 
diagonal, which means that the model is able to correctly 
predict most of the samples.
